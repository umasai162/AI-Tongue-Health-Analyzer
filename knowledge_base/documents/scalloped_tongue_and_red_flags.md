# Clinical Guide: Scalloped Tongue and Critical Red Flag Symptoms
**Source:** Oral Surgery, Oral Medicine, Oral Pathology, Oral Radiology & NICE Clinical Guidelines
**Scope:** Triage Rules and Red Flag Symptoms for Clinical Escalation

## 1. Scalloped Tongue (Crenated Tongue / Lingua Indentata)
- **Clinical Appearance:** Compression depressions and scalloped indentations matching the lingual surfaces of adjacent mandibular teeth along the lateral borders.
- **Etiology:**
  - Macroglossia (true enlargement): Hypothyroidism (myxedematous tongue swelling), amyloidosis, acromegaly.
  - Tongue thrusting, clenching, or nocturnal bruxism: Persistent pressure against dental arches during sleep.
  - Obstructive Sleep Apnea (OSA): Significant clinical correlation where enlarged tongue collides with posterior oropharyngeal airway and dental margins.
  - Dehydration / habit-induced pressure habits.

## 2. Red Flag Symptoms Requiring Urgent Clinical Evaluation
Any user presenting with or inquiring about the following symptoms MUST be strongly directed to consult a dentist, oral maxillofacial surgeon, or ENT specialist immediately:
- **Persistent Ulceration:** Any solitary ulcer lasting longer than 14-21 days without healing.
- **Leukoplakia / Erythroplakia:** Fixed white or velvety red mucosal patches that cannot be wiped or scraped away.
- **Indurated Lumps or Swellings:** Firm, nodular masses within the lingual body or floor of mouth.
- **Unexplained Bleeding:** Spontaneous hemorrhage from tongue tissue upon minimal contact.
- **Dysphagia or Odynophagia:** Progressive difficulty or pain while swallowing.
- **Restricted Tongue Mobility:** Ankyloglossia-like restriction or deviation upon protrusion.
- **Persistent Unilateral Pain or Ear Referral:** Referred pain to the ipsilateral ear without primary otic pathology.
