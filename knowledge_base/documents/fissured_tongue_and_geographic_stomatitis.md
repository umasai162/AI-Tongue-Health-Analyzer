# Clinical Guide: Fissured Tongue and Benign Migratory Glossitis
**Source:** World Health Organization Collaborating Centre for Oral Mucosal Diseases
**Scope:** Structural Variations and Benign Mucosal Patterns

## 1. Fissured Tongue (Lingua Plicata)
- **Clinical Presentation:** Deep or superficial grooves and furrows along the central dorsal midline or radiating across lateral dorsal surfaces.
- **Prevalence & Etiology:** Found in 2% to 5% of the general population. Frequently idiopathic, hereditary (polygenic inheritance), or associated with aging.
- **Systemic Considerations:** High prevalence in Melkersson-Rosenthal syndrome (triad of fissured tongue, recurrent facial palsy, and orofacial granulomatosis) and Down syndrome.
- **Management:** Fissured tongue itself is completely benign and asymptomatic in the vast majority of cases. However, food entrapment within deep crevices can promote localized microbial stagnation, halitosis, or secondary candidal irritation. Regular gentle dorsum brushing with soft bristles and warm water is recommended.

## 2. Benign Migratory Glossitis (Geographic Tongue)
- **Clinical Appearance:** Well-demarcated erythematous depapillated areas bounded by slightly elevated white or yellowish circinate serpentine borders.
- **Dynamic Pattern:** Areas heal and relocate across days to weeks, creating an evolving map-like appearance.
- **Symptoms:** Generally asymptomatic, though spicy, acidic, or salty foods may cause transient burning dysesthesia.
- **Significance:** Benign inflammatory condition linked to stress, atopy, or psoriasis. Does not undergo malignant transformation. Reassurance and symptomatic dietary avoidance are primary care cornerstones.
